2026 Paycheck & Tax Calculator FAQ

How much is take-home pay on a $100,000 salary in a no-income-tax state?
Using official 2026 IRS brackets (Rev. Proc. 2025-32, $16,100 single standard deduction), a single W-2 earner making $100,000 in a 0% state-tax state such as Texas or Florida owes $13,170 in federal income tax and $7,650 in FICA (7.65%), leaving $79,180 per year or $3,045 per biweekly paycheck.
How do FICA taxes (Social Security and Medicare) work on my paycheck?
In 2026, W-2 employees pay 6.2% Social Security tax up to the $184,500 SSA wage base ($11,439 maximum per year) plus 1.45% Medicare tax on all covered wages (7.65% combined). Wages above $200,000 (Single) or $250,000 (Married Filing Jointly) also incur a 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax.
How do pre-tax 401(k) and HSA contributions reduce my paycheck taxes?
Traditional pre-tax 401(k) contributions reduce federal and most state taxable income dollar-for-dollar, though they remain subject to FICA. Payroll HSA contributions reduce federal income tax and FICA, lowering your effective tax rate in higher marginal brackets (22%, 24%, or above).
Which US states have 0% state income tax on W-2 wages?
Nine US states levy 0% state income tax on W-2 wages: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming. Comparing a high-tax state like California or New York against a 0% state often changes net pay by $5,000 to $12,000+ per year.
