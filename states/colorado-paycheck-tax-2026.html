How Colorado Taxes Your Paycheck in 2026

Colorado levies a low, simple flat 4.4% state income tax on W-2 salaries and 1099 freelance earnings in 2026—one of the lowest flat income tax rates in America (and occasionally lowered slightly in years with TABOR surplus refunds). Colorado W-2 employees also contribute a modest 0.45% payroll share toward Colorado's Paid Family and Medical Leave Insurance (FAMLI) program, while Denver workers pay a tiny flat $5.75/month Occupational Privilege Tax rather than a percentage-based city income tax.

Real Take-Home Pay Analysis for Colorado Earners

On a $100,000 salary in Denver, Boulder, or Colorado Springs in 2026, a Single filer pays federal income tax, 7.65% FICA, and roughly $3,960 in Colorado state income tax—taking home approximately $74,830/year (~$6,236/month) before the ~$450/yr FAMLI deduction. Married couples filing jointly at $100,000 keep roughly $80,460/year (~$6,705/month). Because Colorado begins directly from Federal Taxable Income, you automatically receive the full $15,000 Single / $30,000 Married federal standard deduction on your Colorado return as well!

Frequently Asked Questions: Colorado Paycheck & Salary Taxes

How much take-home pay is $100,000 in Colorado (Denver) in 2026?

A Single filer earning $100,000 in Colorado nets approximately $74,830/year (~$6,236/month) after federal income tax, FICA, and the flat 4.4% Colorado state tax (minus ~$450/yr FAMLI).

Does Denver charge a percentage city income tax?

No. Denver only charges a flat $5.75/month Occupational Privilege Tax (head tax) for employees earning over $500/month—never a percentage of your salary.

Does Colorado give you the federal standard deduction on state taxes?

Yes. Because Colorado starts with Federal Taxable Income, the full $15,000 Single / $30,000 Married standard deduction automatically reduces your Colorado taxable income.
