How Florida Taxes Your Paycheck in 2026

Florida charges 0% state personal income tax in 2026 on W-2 paychecks, bonuses, commissions, freelance 1099 income, and retirement distributions. Protected by Florida's state constitution, workers in Miami, Tampa, Orlando, and Jacksonville keep 100% of their paycheck after federal IRS income tax and standard 7.65% FICA withholdings, with zero local city or county wage taxes anywhere in the state.

Real Take-Home Pay Analysis for Florida Earners

A Single earner making $100,000 in Florida takes home roughly $78,790/year (~$6,566/month), while a Married couple filing jointly at $100,000 keeps over $84,420/year (~$7,035/month). Compared to New York City, a Miami resident earning $100,000 keeps an extra $8,000+ per year in spendable cash—and at $200,000, the annual paycheck gap widens to $18,000–$22,000+. That immediate cash-flow boost lets Florida earners max out 401(k)s, Roth IRAs, and brokerage index funds significantly faster than peers in high-tax coastal states.

Frequently Asked Questions: Florida Paycheck & Salary Taxes

What is the take-home pay on $100,000 in Florida in 2026?

A Single filer earning $100,000 in Florida takes home approximately $78,790/year ($6,566/month or $3,030 biweekly) after federal income tax and 7.65% FICA.

Does Miami or Orlando charge a local paycheck tax?

No. Florida has no state income tax and no local municipal wage taxes.

Are bonuses and overtime taxed by Florida?

No. Bonuses and overtime in Florida are subject only to federal income tax withholding and FICA—never state income tax.
