How Michigan Taxes Your Paycheck in 2026

Michigan levies a straightforward flat 4.25% state personal income tax rate on W-2 wages and 1099 income in 2026, paired with personal exemptions ($5,600+ per person). Most Michigan suburbs (Ann Arbor, Troy, Novi, Royal Oak) charge 0% local income tax, though 24 Michigan cities—most notably Detroit (2.4% resident / 1.2% non-resident), Grand Rapids (1.5% / 0.75%), and Lansing (1.0% / 0.5%)—levy a separate municipal wage tax.

Real Take-Home Pay Analysis for Michigan Earners

On a $100,000 salary in Ann Arbor or Metro Detroit suburbs in 2026, a Single filer pays federal income tax, 7.65% FICA, and roughly $3,825 in Michigan state tax—taking home approximately $74,965/year (~$6,247/month or ~$2,883 biweekly). If you live inside the City of Detroit, the 2.4% city resident tax reduces annual net pay by roughly ~$2,350 (or ~$1,180 for suburbanites commuting into downtown Detroit). Michigan also phased out its old pension tax under the 'Lowering MI Costs Plan', improving after-tax retirement income for longtime Michigan residents.

4 Smart Ways to Boost Your Michigan Take-Home Pay in 2026

  • Maximize Traditional 401(k) and HSA deductions—Michigan starts from Federal AGI, so pre-tax workplace deferrals save both federal (22%–24%) and Michigan 4.25% taxes automatically.
  • If you work remotely from a 0%-local-tax suburb for a Detroit-based employer, file Detroit Form 5118/5119 each spring to claim a full refund of non-resident Detroit tax withheld for remote workdays!
  • Claim Michigan's MESP 529 state tax deduction (up to $5,000 Single / $10,000 Married Filing Jointly) to save up to $425/yr in Michigan state tax.
  • Remember Michigan has reciprocal income tax agreements with Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, Minnesota, Ohio, and Wisconsin—paying only Michigan 4.25% if you commute across state lines.

Frequently Asked Questions: Michigan Paycheck & Salary Taxes

How much is $100,000 after taxes in Michigan in 2026?

Outside of local-tax cities, a Single filer earning $100,000 nets roughly $74,965/year (~$6,247/month). Living inside Detroit adds a 2.4% city tax (~$2,350/yr).

Do Ann Arbor or Troy charge a local city income tax?

No. Ann Arbor, Troy, Novi, and most Metro Detroit suburbs charge 0% local personal income tax.

Can remote workers get a refund on Detroit non-resident wage tax?

Yes. Non-residents of Detroit only owe the 1.2% Detroit tax on days physically worked inside Detroit city limits—days worked remotely from home outside Detroit are refundable.
