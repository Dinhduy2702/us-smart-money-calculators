How New Jersey Taxes Your Paycheck in 2026

New Jersey uses a seven-bracket progressive state income tax in 2026 ranging from 1.4% up to 10.75% on taxable income over $1,000,000. For middle-income salaries up to $75,000–$100,000, New Jersey's effective state tax rate is actually quite moderate (~3.8%–4.5%), though marginal rates step up to 6.37% above $75,000 (Single) and 8.97% above $500,000. New Jersey W-2 workers also see small payroll withholdings for NJ Unemployment (SUI), State Disability (SDI), and Family Leave Insurance (FLI).

Real Take-Home Pay Analysis for New Jersey Earners

On a $100,000 gross salary in New Jersey in 2026, a Single filer pays federal income tax, 7.65% FICA, and roughly $4,000 in New Jersey state income tax—leaving approximately $74,800/year (~$6,230/month). One huge quirk for New Jersey paychecks: like California, New Jersey does NOT conform to federal HSA tax deductions (HSA contributions are taxable for NJ state income tax), and Traditional 401(k) contributions ARE tax-exempt in New Jersey—making 401(k) contributions your #1 state tax shield. For NJ residents commuting to Manhattan, NJ grants a credit for NY State taxes paid, and living in NJ avoids the 3.876% NYC city income tax completely.

4 Smart Ways to Boost Your New Jersey Take-Home Pay in 2026

  • Max out Traditional 401(k) contributions first in New Jersey—401(k) deferrals reduce BOTH federal and NJ state taxes, whereas HSAs only reduce federal tax in NJ.
  • If you live in Hoboken, Jersey City, or Bergen County and work in Manhattan, ensure your payroll department is NOT mistakenly withholding NYC local resident tax.
  • Remember New Jersey has a small $1,000 personal exemption (rather than a $15,000 federal standard deduction), so pre-tax 401(k) and health insurance premiums matter even more.
  • Check New Jersey's property tax deduction (up to $15,000 deductible on NJ returns) if you own a home in the state.

Frequently Asked Questions: New Jersey Paycheck & Salary Taxes

How much is $100,000 after taxes in New Jersey in 2026?

A Single filer earning $100,000 in New Jersey takes home roughly $74,820/year (~$6,235/month) after federal income tax, FICA, and NJ state income tax.

Are HSA contributions tax-free in New Jersey?

No. New Jersey and California are the only two US states that tax HSA contributions and earnings at the state level (though HSAs remain 100% federal tax-free).

Do New Jersey residents working in New York pay double state tax?

No. You file a NY non-resident return and a NJ resident return, and NJ credits you for taxes paid to NY—effectively meaning you pay the higher of the two states' rates, not both.
