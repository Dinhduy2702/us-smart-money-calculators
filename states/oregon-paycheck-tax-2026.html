How Oregon Taxes Your Paycheck in 2026

Oregon has one of the steepest state income tax curves in America in 2026—hitting 8.75% at just ~$10,750 of taxable income (Single) and topping out at 9.9% above $125,000—because Oregon charges 0% state sales tax! On top of state income tax, Oregon W-2 employees pay Paid Leave Oregon (~0.6% employee share) and Oregon's Statewide Transit Tax (0.1%). Residents of Portland / Multnomah County earning over $125k Single ($200k Married) also face local Preschool for All (1.5%–3.0%) and Metro Supportive Housing (1.0%) surtaxes.

Real Take-Home Pay Analysis for Oregon Earners

On a $100,000 salary in Portland, Eugene, or Bend in 2026, a Single filer pays federal income tax, 7.65% FICA, and roughly $6,260–$7,500+ in Oregon state income tax—leaving approximately $72,530/year (~$6,044/month) before Paid Leave Oregon (~$600/yr). Note one unique quirk of the Portland–Vancouver metro area: residents who live across the Columbia River in Vancouver, Washington (0% wage tax) and work remotely pay 0% Oregon income tax, whereas commuting physically into Portland triggers Oregon non-resident tax on Oregon workdays!

Frequently Asked Questions: Oregon Paycheck & Salary Taxes

How much take-home pay is $100,000 in Oregon (Portland) in 2026?

A Single filer earning $100,000 in Oregon takes home roughly $72,530/year (~$6,044/month) after federal tax, FICA, and Oregon state income tax (minus ~$700/yr for Paid Leave Oregon & transit tax).

Why is Oregon's state income tax rate so high on a $100,000 salary?

Oregon has 0% sales tax, so the state relies heavily on personal income taxes—reaching the 8.75% bracket at just ~$10,750 of taxable income and 9.9% above $125,000.

Do Washington residents working remotely for an Oregon company pay Oregon tax?

No—non-residents only owe Oregon income tax on days physically worked inside Oregon.
