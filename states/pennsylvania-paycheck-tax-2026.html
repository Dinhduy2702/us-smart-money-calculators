How Pennsylvania Taxes Your Paycheck in 2026

Pennsylvania charges one of the lowest flat state income tax rates in America at just 3.07% in 2026—with ZERO tax on qualified retirement distributions (401(k), IRA, pension, Social Security). However, Pennsylvania has two critical paycheck quirks every worker must know: (1) Pennsylvania does NOT allow a state tax deduction for Traditional 401(k) contributions going IN (taxing them up front at 3.07%, then taxing 0% on withdrawal!), and (2) virtually every PA municipality levies a local Earned Income Tax (EIT)—typically 1.0% in suburbs, 3.0% in Pittsburgh, and up to 3.75% for Philadelphia residents.

Real Take-Home Pay Analysis for Pennsylvania Earners

At the state level alone, a Single filer earning $100,000 in Pennsylvania pays only ~$2,763–$3,070 in PA state tax—leaving roughly $76,027/year (~$6,336/month) before local municipal EIT. If you live or work inside Philadelphia (3.75% resident / ~3.44% non-resident wage tax), that city wage tax trims another ~$3,440–$3,750/year off your take-home pay! Meanwhile, suburban PA commuters who work outside Philadelphia pay only ~1.0% local EIT—keeping net pay among the highest in the Northeast.

4 Smart Ways to Boost Your Pennsylvania Take-Home Pay in 2026

  • Remember Pennsylvania taxes Traditional 401(k) contributions at 3.07% going in AND taxes 0% coming out—making Roth 401(k) vs. Traditional 401(k) purely a federal tax bracket decision in PA!
  • Unlike 401(k)s, Pennsylvania DOES allow pre-tax HSA deductions at the state level—max out your HSA ($4,300+ Single / $8,550+ Family) to cut both federal and PA 3.07% taxes.
  • If you work remotely for a Philadelphia company from outside city limits, confirm whether your employer is required to withhold the Philadelphia non-resident wage tax or if you qualify for a refund on out-of-city days.
  • Take advantage of Pennsylvania's generous 529 deduction (up to $18,000/yr per beneficiary deductible on PA returns—even for out-of-state 529 plans!).

Frequently Asked Questions: Pennsylvania Paycheck & Salary Taxes

How much is $100,000 after taxes in Pennsylvania in 2026?

Before local municipal wage tax, a Single filer earning $100,000 nets roughly $76,027/year (~$6,336/month). Expect ~$1,000/yr less in typical 1% EIT suburbs, or ~$3,750/yr less inside Philadelphia.

Why did my PA state tax withholding not drop when I contributed to my 401(k)?

Pennsylvania is the only US state that taxes employee 401(k) contributions at 3.07% up front—in exchange, Pennsylvania taxes 0% of your 401(k) and IRA withdrawals in retirement.

Does Pennsylvania tax Social Security or pension income?

No. Pennsylvania completely exempts Social Security, pensions, and qualified 401(k)/IRA withdrawals after age 59.5 from state income tax.
