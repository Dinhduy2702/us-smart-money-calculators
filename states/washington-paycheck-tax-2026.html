How Washington Taxes Your Paycheck in 2026

Washington State charges 0% state income tax on W-2 salaries, cash bonuses, and vested RSUs in 2026—making Seattle, Bellevue, and Redmond a powerhouse for high-earning tech compensation. While Washington has no state income tax on wages, W-2 paychecks do include two small state payroll deductions: the WA Cares long-term care fund (0.58% on all wages) and WA Paid Family & Medical Leave (PFML, ~0.6% employee share up to the Social Security wage cap).

Real Take-Home Pay Analysis for Washington Earners

Excluding the ~1.2% combined WA Cares/PFML payroll programs, a Single filer earning $100,000 in Washington pays only federal income tax and 7.65% FICA—netting roughly $78,790/year before WA payroll fees (or ~$77,600/year after WA Cares and PFML). Compared to Silicon Valley (California), a Seattle software engineer earning $150,000–$300,000 saves $12,000 to $30,000+ every single year in state income tax, even on vested Amazon or Microsoft RSUs (which are taxed as ordinary W-2 wages, NOT subject to Washington's 7% capital gains excise tax).

Frequently Asked Questions: Washington Paycheck & Salary Taxes

How much take-home pay is $100,000 in Washington State in 2026?

A Single filer earning $100,000 in Washington nets roughly $78,790/year after federal income tax and FICA (~$77,600/year after WA Cares 0.58% and WA PFML payroll deductions).

Are Amazon or Microsoft RSU vests taxed by Washington's 7% capital gains tax?

No. RSU vests count as ordinary W-2 wages, which face 0% Washington state income tax. Only Long-Term Capital Gains above $270,000/yr from selling stock later face Washington's excise tax.

Does Seattle have a city income tax on paychecks?

No. Seattle does not impose a personal city income tax on employees.
